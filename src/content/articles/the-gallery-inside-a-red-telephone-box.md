In one small town, you can see an entire exhibition in the time it takes to make a phone call you
were never going to make.

## The smallest rooms in art

When the mobile phone made the telephone box redundant, a few communities refused to let the
glass boxes rot. They were cleaned, shelved, and handed to artists: miniature galleries with a
locking door, a tiny hanging rail, and a rotating show that changes without announcement.

No press release. No opening night. You either notice it or you do not.

## What the format does to the work

Scale changes behaviour. Artists who arrive intending to make something small often end up
making something *specific* — because at this size, vagueness has nowhere to hide. A pencil line
has to earn its place when the whole room is a cupboard.

The shows also disappear honestly. Pieces are stolen, rained on, occasionally swapped by
someone who simply preferred their own drawing. The archive of these galleries is a record of
trust as much as art.

## Why they matter

There is a lesson here for anyone who thinks curation requires a white wall and a grant: the
distance between an idea and an audience can be about thirty centimetres.

If your town has an empty phone box, it may already be a gallery. It just hasn't been told yet.
